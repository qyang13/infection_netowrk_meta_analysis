To answer your questions: 

Gene expression networks are kinda weird because they are constructed from correlations e.g. through RNAseq experiments. We gain some insight through knockout/knockdown/recovery experiments, but the network, even in those cases, is really a simplification of complicated dynamics. In that scenario, what does an edge from i to j really mean?  So... it's a challenge! If you know Michael Bradshaw, who works with Ryan Layer, you might get in touch with him. (If not, I can introduce you.)  He has been reviewing this literature for a while. 

I don't think that there's any clear answer to what kind of pairwise association score is best! The sad, complete answer, is "it depends."

What kind of algorithm to use? Hmmm. It depends on what kind of data you have. Weighted or unweighted? Directed or undirected? Strictly positive or signed? I am most impressed with SBM family methods, and Peixoto / Graph-Tool has a weighted SBM too. Not sure what the state of the art is for signed networks. 

The goal should be to use networks—analysis and/or modeling—in service of the scientific questions. So be sure to include various analyses that include networks. But if you were to do a project that included networks as a means to do analyses, that would also be great.
